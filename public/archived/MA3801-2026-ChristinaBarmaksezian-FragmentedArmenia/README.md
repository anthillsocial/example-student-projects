## Overview
Fragmented Armenia is an interactive digital website that explores Armenian identity as fragmented, inherited, and networked, shaped by genocide, displacement, and diaspora.

Rather than presenting a fixed narrative, the website invites the user to interact with a dynamic system. A focal rupture visualises how identity is dispersed into fragments that remain connected through digital networks, reflecting the lived realities of the Armenian diaspora. The interface invited users to explore the Armenian identity through a collection of archival materials, ancestral memories, and personal reflections.

## Concept, Motivation, and Aim
The project showcases that Armenian identity is not rooted in geographical belonging but constructed through constant movement, inherited memory, and diasporic experiences. After being displaced due to the Armenian genocide in 1915, Armenians carried fragments of memory rather than land; dispersed fragments that continue to circulate and connect through digital networks. The interface positions identity as distributed and fluid, forming a diaspora full of unique yet interconnected experiences that define what it means to be Armenian. 

My motivation for this project stems from my personal experience as an Armenian whose identity has been shaped by displacement, migration, and a sense of belonging that transcends geography. My family’s journey began with my ancestors who fled the Armenian Genocide and sought refuge in Aleppo, Syria. In 2012, the outbreak of the Syrian civil war forced us to migrate again. This time, we moved to Germany, where I have lived the longest. This has left me with a complex relationship with the idea of “home.” While Germany is where I have lived the longest, and Armenia is at the core of my cultural identity, neither feels entirely like home. Instead, my sense of self is rooted in movement, inherited memory, and connections maintained across borders, often digitally. Although this project was inspired by my story, its goal is to reflect the shared experiences of millions of Armenians worldwide. Through this work, I aim to highlight the broader concepts of identity, memory, and belonging within a globally dispersed, networked community.

## Structure
The project is a single-page website that undergoes three main stages:

### Origin (Historical Map):
- A hand-drawn map of Armenia’s historical territories pre-genocide
- The interface is intentionally minimal to encourage a moment of observation

### Rupture (Fragmentation):
- The map explodes into dispersed fragments automatically after a short timer
- Represents genocide, displacement, and diaspora formation
- Fragments scatter while remaining connected to a central node

### Network (Connection and Memory):
- A centralised network forms, centred by the Armenian “Forget-Me-Not” flower
- Nodes display map fragments, handwritten notes, and archival images
- Users can interact with the handwritten notes, archives, and the flower in the centre
- Showcases the unique yet shared experiences and memories within the diaspora while mirroring how they stay connected through digital networks

## Technologies Used
- HTML: basic page structure
- CSS: control layout, background, canvas behaviour
- JavaScript: interaction logic, asset loading, and project behaviour
- p5.js: canvas rendering, animation, image display, and network generation
- Google Forms: to gather notes and archives from contributors

## Key Features
- Rupture of a map into a networked system
- Dynamic node generation representing dispersed fragments
- Integration of handwritten notes and archival imagery
- Click-based exploration of personal stories and memories
- Animated message bubbles for archival narratives
- Central symbolic node (Forget-Me-Not flower) as a point of convergence
Intent
- Subtle hover cues, with elements expanding or illuminating to signal interactivity

## Notes
- The archival material is intentionally partial and selective, reflecting the incomplete nature of memory and history
- Handwritten contributions emphasise personal voices over institutional narratives
- The system prioritises emotional and experiential engagement over factual completeness

### Reason why I chose flower as the the centre of the map (copy-pasted from online):
The Armenian forget-me-not (known as anmoṙuk in Armenian) is a potent national symbol, officially adopted for the Armenian Genocide Centennial commemoration in 2015 to represent eternal remembrance. 
Symbolism and Meaning:
Every part of the Armenian forget-me-not emblem carries deep historical and cultural significance related to the Armenian Genocide of 1915: 
The Name: The flower's name itself is a call to "remember and demand" justice, ensuring the victims and their stories are never forgotten.
The Black Center: This part represents the horrors, pain, and dark memories of the Genocide.
The Yellow Core/Inner Area: The golden inner area symbolizes light, hope, and the sun, picturing the 12 pillars of the Tsitsernakaberd Armenian Genocide Memorial in Yerevan.
The Five Petals: The five petals represent the five continents where Armenian survivors found refuge and created the widespread Armenian diaspora communities across the world.
The Purple Color: As the dominant color, purple is traditionally associated with Armenian identity and the vestments of the Armenian Church, symbolizing the nation's spiritual strength, condemnation, and recognition of the genocide, which continues to survive despite the tragedy.
