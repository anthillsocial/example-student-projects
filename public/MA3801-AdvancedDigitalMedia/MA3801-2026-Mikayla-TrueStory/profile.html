There are no shortcuts to improving equine welfare.

In May 2018 the final Hallelujah Horses were placed in adopted homes or rescue organisations; however the completion of the two year case does not imply an end to equine mistreatment and neglect. 70% of United States equine rescues operate at over capacity to satisfy the constant need for increased animal welfare.

About the Project

Prompted to create a digital media piece as a final year university project, Mikayla Billin founded the ideas of Francisco based upon her experience with a Hallelujah Horse of the same name.

A digital exploration of a true story, Francisco recounts the largest equine rescue mission in the United States through a blend of traditional and interactive storytelling from the horse’s perspective. This innovative viewpoint combined with statements of hard hitting facts and unfiltered content employs a way to raise awareness of animal and equine welfare, a topic generally overlooked to the public, while avoiding stereotypes that currently exist around the subject. With the rescue mission highly reliant on forms of online communication, the project utilises social media style engagement to portray its importance as well as to investigate the recent shift in digital ways of thinking. The project aims to merge digital mannerisms and narration methods to create an interactive exploration that contradicts stereotypical animal welfare campaigns to generate awareness and community-style involvement both online and off.

It is Mikayla's hope that the project will continue to develop and encourage audiences to participate in the creation of a communcal space advocating for animal and equine welfare, while also showing appreciation for both horses and humans involved in the Hallelujah Horses rescue case.
